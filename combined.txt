The internet has vastly changed from its original intent during cold war as a defense network but now has become the foundation of modern society, connecting billions of devices across commerce, education and especially daily communication. Early development before the 1960s, computing relied on mainframes, and telecommunications relied on centralized telephone lines being circuit switched, this was horribly inefficient for computer data while being very vulnerable to single point failures. To combat this Paul Baran at the RAND corperation and Donald Davies at the uk's national physics laboratory had developed the concept of packet switching which means breaking down data into small block and routing that data into independently across shared lines. Lead by J.C.R Licklider's vision of interconnected computers in a network. The u.s. advanced research projects agency had launched arpanet in 1969, connecting four universities with the specific goal of reliably sharing research.

Key milestones:

#1969) arpnet launch: created by Bolt Beranek and Newman with the theoretical foundations by leonard klienrock. This would prove the practicality of packet switching

#1974 - 1983) TCP/IP: vint cerf and bob kahn codesigned the transmission conntrol protocol and internet protocol. Enabling separate networks to merge as a single network.

#1989 - 1991 the world web: At cern, tim berners lee invented HTTP, HTML, and URLs releasing them freely with no royalties attached, tansforming the raw internet infrastructure into a navigable hypertext system

# 2004 - present day: with the introduction of smartphones, social platforms and cloud computing made the in grow into a real time, mobile first ecosystem.

Beyond hardware and protocol upgrades, the cultural and economic model of the internet went through a fundamental shift during the mid 2000s with the rise of web 2.0. With the adoption of smartphones following 2007, this transition transformed internet access from being a stationary desktop experience into a portable easily accessed necessity of everyday life.

Over five decades, the internet has transitioned from a text based academic backbone into a high speed fiber optic and 5G utility. Thanks to innovations such as domain names and graphical interfaces it brought the removal of daunting technical barriers. And competitive internet service providers lowered costs. Scientific


The scientific development of the Internet is about how researchers solved the problem of getting computers to share data, first with packet switching and the ARPANET, then with TCP (Transmission control protocol)/IP(Internet protocol), and the World Wide Web. packet switching is where data is broken into small packets that can be sent separately, and this was tested in the ARPANET, which connected its first four computers in 1969. After this, Robert Kahn started working on TCP/IP in 1972, which is a set of rules that let separate networks communicate with each other and this is what allowed the ARPANET to grow into a network of networks. The World Wide Web came later as one of the applications built on this foundation , which was possible because TCP/IP was designed as a general infrastructure and not for one specific purpose (Leiner et al., 1997).

The idea of packet switching was confirmed necessary in 1965, when Lawrence Roberts connected two computers on opposite sides of the US and found that the phone system was not good enough for the job. And at the same time researchers at MIT, RAND and the UK national Physical Laboratory were developing the same idea without knowing about each other. Roberts published his plan for the ARPANET in 1967 and the company Bolt Beranek and Newman(BBN) was chosen to build the packet switches called Interface Message Processors, that would connect the computers to the network. The first of these switches was installed at UCLA in September 1969 and a month later the first message between two computers on the network was sent from UCLA to the Stanford Research Institute. By the end of 1970 the first set of rules for the computers on the network to communicate, called the Network Control Protocol(NCP), was finished and once sites had installed it in 1971 and 1972, people could start building applications like email (Leiner et al., 1997). 

The ARPANET's first protocol, NCP, relied on the network never losing data and could not reach other networks, which is why Kahn needed a new design. In 1973, Kahn asked Vint Cerf, who had worked on the ARPANET's first protocol, to help with the detailed design of TCP/IP. The original TCP was reorganised into two protocols after early work on packet voice showed that, for some applications, it was better to leave lost packets to the application than to correct them. IP was left to handle the addressing and forwarding of packets, while TCP made sure data arrived reliably. By 1990 when the ARPANET was shut down, TCP/IP had replaced or sidelined most other wide-area network protocols around the world (Leiner et al., 1997). 

Tim Berners-Lee invented the World Wide Web in 1989 while working at CERN, because scientists around the world needed an easier way to share information with each other. By the end of 1990 he had the first web server and browser running and in 1991 he released the software and announced it on internet newsgroups, which spread interest in it around the world. In 1993 the Mosaic browser made the web much easier to use and CERN made the web's source code free for anyone to use, which helped it spread quickly (CERN, n.d.).

The scientific viewpoint is about how the Internet was built, while the societal viewpoint explains why it spread, since email, which was only meant to help developers to co-ordinate ended up being what most people used it for. Overall, the internet came from scientific research but it only became what it is today because people found uses for it that the researchers had not planned for (Leiner et al., 1997).

For example, when affordable connectivity lost its funding in 2024, about 13% of its users cancelled their internet straight away.
