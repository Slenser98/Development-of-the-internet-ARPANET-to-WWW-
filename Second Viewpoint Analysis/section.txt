second view point analysis - Scientific


Scientific development of the Internet is the way how scientists find the solution on how to make the computers share data through the packet switching and ARPANET. Then the TCP (Transmission control protocol)/IP(Internet protocol) and World Wide Web. Packet switching is the method for dividing data into small packets, which may be sent individually. The first 4 computers of the ARPANET were connected in 1969 to test the packet switching. Later, in 1972, Robert Kahn began to work on TCP/IP. It's the set of rules that allows different networks to talk to each other. This led to the expansion of the ARPANET into a network of networks. Later, one of the applications that emerged on top of that was the World Wide Web. Which was possible because of TCP/IP was designed to be general infrastructure not meant to do anything particular (Leiner et al., 1997).

In 1965, Lawrence Roberts needed to link two computers on opposite sides of US together and discovered that telephone system wasn't up to the task. Hence the need of packet switching was confirmed. It was during this time that a group of researchers in MIT, RAND and UK national Physical lab were all developing the same thing without even knowing about each other. In 1967 Roberts published his plan for ARPANET. Bolt Beranek and Newman(BBN) company was selected to design the packet switches (Interface Message Processors) that will connect the computers to the network. The first one of them was installed in UCLA in September of 1969. And a month later first message to and from two computers on the network was sent from UCLA to Stanford Research Institute. At the end of 1970 first set of rules how computers on the network should talk to each other was developed(Network Control Protocol(NCP), was finished and once sites had installed it in 1971 and 1972, people could start building applications like email (Leiner et al., 1997). 

The very first protocol for the ARPANET (NCP) assumes data is not lost (and no other network), so Kahn had to come up with new design. Cerf (who was involved with first protocol of the ARPANET) was asked by Kahn in 1973 to assist with the detailed design of TCP/IP. Original TCP was divided into two different protocols after initial work on packet voice was done and it was determined that sometimes it was better to let applications deal with lost packets instead of trying to fix it (in some apps). IP was left with dealing with packet addressing and forwarding of the packets. While TCP ensured reliable delivery of data. Most other protocols across the globe were replaced or became irrelevant in 1990, when ARPANET was shutdown, by TCP/IP. (Leiner et al., 1997)

Tim Berners-Lee developed World Wide Web in 1989 while working at CERN to help all the scientist around the world to easily share information with each other. 1990 end of the year he had first web server and web browser running. Software was released in 1991 and announced on internet newsgroupers to create worldwide interest. In 1993, web was made much more usable with Mosaic browser. Also, CERN made web's source code freely available for use which helped with the rapid dissemination of the web (CERN, n.d.)

Scientific point of view is that of the construction of the Internet. Whereas the social point of view is for the spreading of the internet as it is the email (which was supposed to help developers only for co-ordinating) became the main use for the internet. Overall, Internet was developed out of scientific research. However, it only became what it is today as people used it for things that the scientists didn't plan for. (Leiner et al., 1997)
