second view point analysis - Scientific


The scientific development of the Internet is about how researchers solved the problem of getting computers to share data, first with packet switching and the ARPANET, then with TCP/IP and the world wide web. packet switching is where data is broken into small packets that can be sent separately, and this was tested in the ARPANET, which connected its first four computers in 1969 (Leiner et al., 1997). After this Robert Kahn started working on TCP/IP in 1972, which is a set of rules that let separate networks communicate with each other and this is what allowed the ARPANET to grow into a network of networks (Leiner et al., 1997). The World Wide Web came later as one of the applications built on this foundation , which was possible because TCP/IP was designed as a general infrastructure and not for one specific purpose (Leiner et al., 1997).

The idea of packet switching was confirmed necessary in 1965, when Lawrence Roberts connected two computers on opposite sides of the US and found that the phone system was not good enough for the job. And at the same time researchers at MIT, RAND and the UK national Physical Laboratory were developing the same idea without knowing about each other (Leiner et al., 1997). Roberts published his plan for the ARPANET in 1967 and the company BBN was chosen to build the packet switches called interface message processors, that would connect the computers to the network. The first of these switches was installed at UCLA in September 1969 and a month later the first message between two computers on the network was sent from UCLA to the Stanford Research Institute (Leiner et al., 1997). By the end of 1970 the first set of rules for the computers on the network to communicate, called the network of control protocol, was finished and oncesites had installed it in 1971 and 1972, people could start building applications like email (Leiner et al., 1997). 

The ARPANET's first protocol, NCO, relied on the network never losing data and could not reach other networks, which is why Kahn needed a new design (Leiner et al., 1997). In 1973, kahn asked vint cerf, who had worked on the ARPANET's first protocol, to help with the details design of TCP/TIP. The original TCP was reorganised inot two protocols after early work on the packet voice showed that, for some applications, it was better to leave lost packets to the application than to correct them (Leiner et al., 1997).
