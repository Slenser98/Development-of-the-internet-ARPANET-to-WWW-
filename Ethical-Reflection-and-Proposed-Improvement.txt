Ethical Reflection

Digital divide:

During the past decade, a fair amount of research on digital divide surfaced. Many reports and studies providing statistical dada (Digital Divide Network, 2002, NTIA, 1995, 1998, 1999, 2000).

With other studies suggesting ways to which digital divide could be understood if it were "reconceptualised" in terms of other metaphors, e.g. a "divide" having something to do with other things such as literacy(Warschauer, 2002), power (Moss, 2002), content (Garvin, 2000), or the information environment (Floridi, 2001).

Authors tented not to question (at least not directly) whether the digital divide is at large is an ethical issue. Many others believe that because of involving access to computing technology exist, issues surrounding the digital divide are needed moral in nature. Many also further assume that this divide has to do with something that is digital and technological in principle, it's better viewed as a computer ethical issue

Reference:

Tavani, H. T. (2003). Ethical reflections on the digital divide. Journal of Information, Communication and Ethics in Society, 1(2), 99–108. https://doi.org/10.1108/14779960380000230
